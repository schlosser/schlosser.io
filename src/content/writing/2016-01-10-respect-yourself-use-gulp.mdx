Making websites just got a lot easier. When it comes to web development, there's a lot to learn.

Maybe someone has told you to use [vendor prefixes](http://css-snippets.com/browser-prefix/) for [cross-browser compatibility](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2010/06/the-principles-of-cross-browser-css-coding/), to [minify your code](https://developers.google.com/web/fundamentals/performance/optimizing-content-efficiency/optimize-encoding-and-transfer?hl=en) to decrease load time, or to use a linter so that your [CSS](http://csslint.net/) and [JavaScript](http://jshint.com/) follows best practices. Maybe someone has ranted about how [using SCSS is better than using CSS](http://www.webdesignerdepot.com/2014/08/5-reasons-you-should-be-using-sass-today/), or about why [templating engines are all the rage right now](http://www.creativebloq.com/web-design/templating-engines-9134396). Or maybe this is the first you're hearing of it, and you have no idea what setup to use.

**Trying to figure out "the right way" can be intimidating.** Respect your sanity and your time: Gulp will save both.

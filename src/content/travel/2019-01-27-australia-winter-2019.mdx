<section class="text">
  <p>
    A year ago, I visited Sydney for the first time. On that trip, given the
    first opportunity to explore that part of the world, I bolted [straight to
    New Zealand](/travel/australia-new-zealand-spring-2018/). While I don't
    regret the decision, I had to put off seeing more of Australia until my next
    visit.
  </p>
  <p>
    This Winter, I got that opportunity. After spending a week in Sydney for
    work, I met up with my friends Lily and Yih-Jen and we spent 10 days
    exploring the country. We hit the big tourist spots in Sydney, took a day
    trip to the Blue Mountains, and explored Tasmania. On our way home, we
    stopped in Melbourne for a short first visit.
  </p>
</section>

    hotel, and took my first photos.

<section class="text" id="mona">
  <p>
    That night, we drove back to Hobart, where we spent the rest of the day. We
    had planned a third day on Bruny Island, but, fearing getting stuck on the
    wrong side of the ferry during a forecasted thunderstorm, we canceled.
    Instead, we made impromptu plans to visit the Museum of Old and New Art
    (MONA). While the thunderstorm didn't really pan out to be much more than a
    drizzle, this was absolutely the right call.
  </p>
  <p>
    I quickly fell in love with MONA for it's architecture. Built mostly
    underground into the side of a hill, the building is at once unremarkable
    from the outside and stunning on the inside. Even more impressive was the
    exhibition and curation. My favorite pieces were several by James Turell, an
    American MacArthur Genius Grant-winning artist that works mostly with light
    and space. I first discovered him when I saw his [site-specific installation
    at MOMA
    PS1](/writing/2018-04-01-australia-new-zealand-spring-2018/#moma-ps1).
    <em>Meeting</em> at MOMA PS1 in Long Island City. (Others may know him for
    his work creating the colorful rooms in Drake's [Hotline Bling music
    video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uxpDa-c-4Mc).) Unbeknownst to me, he
    also has several installations at MONA in Hobart, including{" "}
    <em>Event Horizon</em>, a room whose walls, ceiling, and floor all meld
    together into one seemingly never-ending space. His artworks rarely travel
    to other museums, and others do not allow photography, making me feel even
    more fortunate that we were able to make the trip to visit.
  </p>
</section>
